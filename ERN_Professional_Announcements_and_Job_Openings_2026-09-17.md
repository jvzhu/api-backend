## Asian Economic Development Conference 2027 — Call for Papers & Participants

**Organizers:** Asian Development Bank (ADB), Asian Development Bank Institute (ADBI), and Lee Kuan Yew School of Public Policy (LKYSPP)  
**Conference:** 6th Asian Economic Development Conference  
**Dates:** July 1–2, 2027  
**Location:** Lee Kuan Yew School of Public Policy, Singapore  
**Paper Submission Deadline:** January 15, 2027

### Description

The Asian Development Bank (ADB), Asian Development Bank Institute (ADBI), and Lee Kuan Yew School of Public Policy (LKYSPP) will hold the 6th Asian Economic Development Conference on July 1–2, 2027 at LKYSPP, Singapore.

The conference brings together researchers, development practitioners, and policymakers to advance frontier research and exchange knowledge on key economic development issues in developing Asia and the Pacific.

The organizers invite submissions of **unpublished, original** empirical research, survey articles, economic modelling, impact evaluations, and policy-based studies.

### Topics

Submissions may address a broad range of development-economics topics, including:

- **Agriculture and Rural Development:** food security, agricultural productivity, market dynamics.
- **Health and Education:** access to health care and education, aging populations, youth and demographic transitions.
- **Labor:** skills development, future of work, job security and quality, labor productivity.
- **Gender:** income and opportunity gaps, gender empowerment.
- **Poverty, Inequality, and Welfare:** income gaps, income shocks, social safety nets, MSMEs, intra-household dynamics, social development.
- **Climate Change and Environment:** mitigation and adaptation, natural capital, sustainable development, net-zero transition, carbon pricing, energy transition, natural disasters.
- **Macroeconomics and Finance:** economic growth, capital markets, sustainable finance, inflation, macroeconomic and financial stability, fiscal and monetary policy.
- **Regional Cooperation and Economic Integration:** trade, economic and financial integration, global value chains, migration, capital flows, cross-border investment.
- **Infrastructure:** energy, transport, water, urban development, quality infrastructure.
- **Digital Economy, Data and Innovation:** digital transformation, technology and innovation, entrepreneurship, digital currencies and assets, data science.
- **Domestic Resource Mobilization and Governance:** public finance, debt sustainability, public management, institutional quality, taxation.
- **Private Sector Development and Industrial Policy:** firm dynamics, entrepreneurship, value chains, innovation in SMEs.
- **Behavioral and Experimental Methods:** RCTs, field and lab experiments, impact evaluations, behavioral insights and nudges.
- **Long-run Development and Economic History:** economic institutions, deep roots, industrialization.

### Submission

Interested authors should register through the conference platform and submit a paper by **January 15, 2027**.

[Conference Registration and Submission](https://adb.eventsair.com/asian-economic-development-conference-2027)

Only complete papers accompanied by an abstract will be considered. Submissions will be evaluated based on originality, analytical rigor, and policy relevance within the Asian context.

## HKIMR Monetary Research Programmes — Call for Proposals

**Organization:** Hong Kong Institute for Monetary and Financial Research (HKIMR)  
**Affiliation:** Hong Kong Academy of Finance (AoF); affiliate of the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA)  
**Application Deadline:** September 30, 2026  
**Expected Notification:** January/February 2027

### Description

HKIMR is the research arm of the Hong Kong Academy of Finance and an affiliate of the Hong Kong Monetary Authority. Its objective is to conduct high-quality research in economics and finance of strategic importance to Hong Kong and the Asia-Pacific region.

HKIMR invites academics and researchers to submit research proposals to its **Monetary Research Programmes**:

- **Regular Research Programme**
- **Thematic Research Programme**

Applicants decide the research topic under the Regular Research Programme. Proposals for the Thematic Research Programme must address the Institute's current thematic areas of interest.

[Regular Research Programme](https://www.aof.org.hk/research/HKIMR/monetary-research-programmes/regular-research-programme)  
[Thematic Research Programme](https://www.aof.org.hk/research/HKIMR/monetary-research-programmes/thematic-research-programme)

### Typical Applicant Profile

Applicants typically:

- Hold a PhD in economics or finance.
- Hold an academic position.
- Have specialization in relevant priority areas.
- Have a publication record in top peer-reviewed academic journals.

Good knowledge of the Asian region and experience in empirical and applied research are additional advantages.

### Support and Expectations

Successful applicants receive:

- An honorarium reflecting the seniority and research qualifications of the researcher.
- Reimbursement of travel and accommodation expenses in Hong Kong for a short visit to the Institute.

Research must be completed within **6 months** from a mutually agreed commencement date.

Completion of a research project requires:

1. Submission of a paper and executive summary for publication in the HKIMR working paper series, following external and internal review.
2. A presentation of the proposed research through a seminar.

Papers produced partly or wholly under the visiting research programme, as well as future versions including working papers and published papers, must acknowledge HKIMR financial support.

The call is **not a grant application**. Requests for research support, data purchase, and multi-year research work will not be entertained.

Successful applicants will be notified in **January/February 2027**.

**Contact:** [hkimr@hkma.gov.hk](mailto:hkimr@hkma.gov.hk)

**Address:** 22/F, International Commerce Centre, 1 Austin Road West, West Kowloon, Hong Kong

## KKCFR — Call for Research Proposals

**Organization:** Kroner and Koenigsberger Center for Financial Research (KKCFR), University of California San Diego  
**Founded:** 2019  
**Proposal Deadline:** December 1, 2026  
**Submission Contact:** Joe Sturtevant, [jsturtevant@ucsd.edu](mailto:jsturtevant@ucsd.edu)

### Overview

The Kroner and Koenigsberger Center for Financial Research (KKCFR) seeks independent academic research proposals on under-researched but important topics in asset management.

The Center aims to improve dialogue between major asset owners—including sovereign wealth funds, pension funds, foundations and their beneficiaries—and the academic community.

Benefits include financial support for new research, access to new datasets and tools shared by member asset owners/CIOs, and opportunities to present research and receive feedback from industry experts.

### Topic 1: A General Framework for an AI Risk Factor

The call identifies AI as a major force shaping equity and credit markets and notes the absence of a canonical AI exposure definition, agreed method for estimating factor loadings, and systematic account of how AI exposure has emerged and intensified.

Research is invited that would:

- Build an economically meaningful framework for identifying firms exposed to the AI investment cycle.
- Estimate stock-level AI factor loadings using a credible identification strategy.
- Trace the evolution of exposure as AI investment, financing, competition, suppliers, and beneficiaries change.
- Assess portfolio concentration after properly measuring AI exposure rather than relying solely on sector or asset-class labels.
- Translate an AI factor into practical concentration-risk management such as position sizing, hedging, or scenario-based stress testing.

#### Research Questions of Interest

- How can firms be mapped into an economically meaningful AI-exposure network covering infrastructure funders, suppliers, financing firms, and productivity beneficiaries?
- What identification strategy yields defensible stock-level AI factor loadings, and how does AI exposure transmit through returns, volatility, earnings revisions, and credit spreads?
- How has the AI factor emerged and intensified, and how have firm- and industry-level exposures shifted?
- Do apparently diversified portfolios remain concentrated in the same underlying AI exposure after measurement?
- How should asset owners apply a formal AI risk factor to concentration-risk management?
- How should public pension funds address risk and index-concentration decisions when AI-related returns and market capitalizations are highly concentrated?

#### Expected Contributions

- Structural classification of firms' economic exposure to the AI investment cycle.
- Formal AI risk factor with estimated and testable loadings, benchmarked against traditional factors.
- Evidence on how the factor's strength and composition evolve over time.
- Practical guidance for institutional concentration-risk management.

### Topic 2: AI Disruption Risk and Resilience

This topic distinguishes AI investment-cycle exposure from a separate form of risk: vulnerability of business models, occupations, and industries to AI-driven disruption.

Research is invited that develops an integrated framework for measuring, scoring, and monitoring AI-driven concentration and disruption risk across institutional portfolios.

#### Research Questions of Interest

- What methodology can measure a firm's or security's sensitivity to AI-driven disruption, and does the factor add explanatory power alongside traditional risk factors?
- What criteria can classify global public and private assets along an AI-vulnerable to AI-resilient spectrum, incorporating labor-force and structural-economic exposure?
- How can statistical and narrative data—including earnings calls, policy announcements, news, and market data—generate real-time probabilistic estimates of macro regimes or disruption scenarios?
- How can a disruption-risk factor, portfolio resilience score, and forward-looking scenario engine be integrated into a continuously updated risk-management system?
- How does AI disruption concentration risk differ from, and interact with, AI capital-spending concentration risk?

#### Expected Contributions

- Validated methodology for measuring stock-level AI-disruption sensitivity.
- Portfolio resilience scoring framework for public and private global assets.
- Real-time AI-assisted scenario and regime-probability tool combining statistical and narrative inputs.
- Demonstrated integration of the three components on a representative institutional portfolio.

### Topic 3: U.S. Treasury Bonds as a Portfolio Diversifier

The third topic asks whether U.S. Treasury bonds continue to provide reliable diversification for long-horizon institutional portfolios, particularly in light of changing fiscal, monetary, geopolitical, and global-investor conditions.

Research is invited that assesses empirically and theoretically whether U.S. government debt remains a reliable portfolio diversifier for U.S. and international investors and identifies conditions under which its diversification properties hold or fail.

#### Research Questions of Interest

- Do U.S. Treasuries continue to show the low or negative bond-equity correlation associated with effective diversification across historical and recent regimes?
- Are there fiscal, monetary, or market conditions—including rising deficits, auction-driven supply shocks, and elevated inflation uncertainty—under which Treasury diversification fails?
- Does diversification value differ between U.S.-domestic and international investors after accounting for currency risk, reserve-currency dynamics, and geopolitical alignment?
- Is the historical tendency of the U.S. dollar to strengthen during global stress still intact, and what would a change imply for Treasuries' safe-haven role?
- If Treasury diversification weakens in certain states of the world, what alternative or complementary diversifying assets should investors consider?

#### Expected Contributions

- Updated empirical assessment of Treasury-equity and Treasury-currency correlations and their dependence on fiscal and geopolitical conditions.
- Framework for identifying conditions under which Treasury diversification is likely to fail.
- Comparative analysis from U.S. and international investor perspectives.
- Practical guidance on complementary or substitute diversifying assets.

### Submission Process

Researchers should submit a proposal addressing **one of the three topics**, either in part or in total.

Proposals will be reviewed by a committee drawn from the KKCFR Academic Advisory Committee, chaired by **Professor Allan Timmermann**.

The goal is to fund **three projects**, preferably no more than one from each topic, although multiple proposals from one topic may be funded.

Successful proposals are expected to lead to novel research outcomes suitable for submission to top academic journals and potentially relevant to investment-industry practice.

### Proposal Requirements

Proposals should be **no longer than 5 pages** and describe:

- Research idea.
- Expected methodology.
- Data requirements.

Related working papers or publications may be included. Principal and co-investigators should submit updated CVs.

**Submit to:** Joe Sturtevant at [jsturtevant@ucsd.edu](mailto:jsturtevant@ucsd.edu)  
**Deadline:** December 1, 2026

### Benefits and Expectations for Winners

KKCFR can provide:

- Summer support for faculty.
- Research assistant support.
- Assistance procuring datasets.
- Up to **$75,000 total** in support.
- Expenses for Center meetings with members of the CIO Advisory Council, Academic Advisory Council, and Industry Advisory Council.

KKCFR is a nonprofit organization and cannot pay university overhead or administrative fees.

Funded researchers are expected to:

- Provide a brief written quarterly research update.
- Be available to present brief research updates at semi-annual Advisory Council meetings, virtually or in person.

The next semi-annual meeting is scheduled for **January 27, 2027, in person in San Diego**.

The University of St.Gallen and its School of Economics and Political Science invite applications for an Assistant Professorship (Tenure Track) in **macroeconomics**. Candidates with a focus on **applied macroeconomics** are particularly encouraged to apply.
